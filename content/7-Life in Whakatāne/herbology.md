# Fireleaf Pepper
A small, bright red pepper with a unique smoky, spicy flavor and high heat level. Known for its medicinal properties and ability to thrive in the volcanic soil around the Shards, Fireleaf Pepper seeds are highly prized by herbalists and chefs alike.
![[herbology-fireleaf-pepper.jpg]]
# Stonebloom Root
A hardy root vegetable with a grayish skin and vibrant purple flesh, often used as a nutrient-dense food source by settlers. Its unique mineral-rich flavor and high nutritional value make it an essential crop, especially valuable for homesteaders in remote areas.
![[herbology-stonebloom-root.jpg]]
# Skybell
A delicate herb with pale blue flowers, known for producing a calming tea. Indigenous to Whakatāne’s southern valleys, it’s particularly valuable because of its soothing effects and mild medicinal properties. The herb is also difficult to cultivate outside its natural habitat, increasing demand for its seeds.
![[herbology-skybell.jpg]]
# Ironstem Fern
A rare fern with a tough, woody stalk that grows well in rocky terrains. Known for its resilience, the Ironstem can be used for fibers in rope-making or woven mats, and settlers have found that its stems are highly resistant to pests, making it valuable for practical applications.
![[herbology-ironstem-fern.jpg]]
# Ashleaf Clover
A native clover with silvery-green leaves, known for its ability to enrich the soil with nitrogen. Kapama farmers use it to improve soil quality and as forage for livestock. Due to its rarity and specialized growing needs, Ashleaf seeds are highly sought after by both farmers and naturalists.
![[herbology-ashleaf-clover.jpg]]
# Mistshade Orchid
A small, dark green orchid with black-veined leaves and dusky purple flowers. Known for its beauty, the Mistshade is also valued by healers, as it produces a rare resin that has wound-healing properties. Difficult to cultivate outside Whakatāne.
![[herbology-mistshade-orchid.jpg]]
# Mercywood Cypress
Mercywood Cypress is a distinctive tree native to the swamps of Whakatāne, thriving in waterlogged soils and shaded, humid environments. Its striking bark, a mix of deep black and yellow streaks, sets it apart from other swamp flora Its slender, towering form is adorned with sparse, dark green needles. The bark contains compounds with mild narcotic properties, providing pain relief when properly processed.
![[herbology-mercywood-cypress.jpg]]
