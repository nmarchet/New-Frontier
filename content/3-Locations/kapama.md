**Population:** 300-400<br>
**Founded:** Year 7 (1831 DE)<br>
**Status:** Incorporated<br>
**Trade Goods, Resources:** Agriculture, textiles, leather products, pottery

Kapama is located in the fertile grasslands around the Vein River and River Romul. Established just a few years into the settlement of Whakatāne, Kapama has grown into the colony's primary source of food and agricultural products. This thriving settlement is characterized by its vast expanses of farmland, where settlers grow a variety of crops—wheat, barley, corn, potatoes and various vegetables—and raise livestock—sheep, goats and chickens—that sustain the colony. 

Kapama sits in a broad, open dale surrounded by gentle hills. Dozens of farms with barns and other outbuildings are scattered around the valley, connected by well-worn paths that converge on the central town of Kapama. The farms are a patchwork of fields neatly sectioned off by wooden fences, with rows of wheat, barley and vegetables growing. In some fields, goats and chickens graze.

The town center is a few blocks of buildings nestled around a crossroads where the Haven Road intersects with the Farm-to-Market Road. Shops and small workshops and houses make up most of the buildings. A few larger buildings stand out―gathering places or storehouses.
# Notable Buildings
**Agricultural Hall.** The headquarters for the local farming cooperative, where farmers gather to trade, discuss best practices and collaborate on large-scale projects. When it’s not in-use for meetings, the co-op rents out the hall for events—a major source of income for the co-op championed by the new president, John McMurray. However, President McMurray is very selective about which musical events are allowed to rent the hall. He is very insistent that “agricultural halls are for agricultural music.”

**Farmer’s Market.** The yard behind the agricultural hall, where farmers gather on the weekend to sell their produce, livestock and homemade goods to locals and visitors from other settlements.

**Grace’s Clayworks.** Grace Bellamy, a skilled potter, sells beautifully glazed pottery and clay figurines. Her signature pieces include water jugs and bowls adorned with designs inspired by the island’s flora and fauna.

**Harlan’s Fine Leathers.** Harlan Wick, a leathermaker, showcases finely crafted belts, boots, saddles, and pouches made from local hides. His store is adorned with strips of leather in various shades, and he occasionally takes custom orders for more specialized gear.

**Kapama Dairy.** A facility where milk from sheep and goats is mass-processed into cheese, butter and other dairy products. It is owned and operated by [Dell Taggart](dell-taggart), who also grows feed crops on several surrounding fields.

**Panacea Clinic.** An outpost where Kapama residents can get regular healthcare. This [Panacea](panacea) clinic also is a base of operations for expeditions to the south. Promising samples are returned here to be packaged for transport to [Fairhaven](fairhaven).
# Major Landmarks
**Kapama Grain Silos (The Grainery).** Several large silos line the road to Fairhaven. They are used for storing grain and other crops before they are transported to other parts of the colony. The facility also houses the seed library for the the Kapama Farmers’ Collective, which contains seeds for several rare native species. It is run by [Emmett Harlow](emmett-harlow).

**Kapama Windmill.** An iconic structure used for grinding grain into flour. A recent visitor from the [Wayfinders’ Union](the-wayfinders-union) named [Ivor Nyberg](ivor-nyberg) paid Old Man Eggers to attach all manner of equipment to the windmill’s drive shaft. He talked about generating something called electricity.

**Twin River Bridge.** A sturdy wooden bridge that spans the Vein River and River Romul where they merge outside of Kapama. It makes a direct connection between Kapama and [Glendale](glendale) possible.
# Lodging
**The Harvest Inn.** A cozy inn near the center of Kapama that offers comfortable rooms and hearty meals made from local ingredients. It's a popular spot for travelers and visiting merchants.

**The Commons.** Just outside of town, these fields have become the place where folks set up camp or park a wagon for a short stay in town. Farmhands and laborers who can’t afford formal accommodations also pitch tents here or just sleep under the stars. A traveling merchant named [Anya Greer](anya-greer) sells camp sundries from her wagon.
